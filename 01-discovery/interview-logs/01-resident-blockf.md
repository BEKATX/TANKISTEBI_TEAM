# Interview 01 · Resident, Block F 3rd Floor · (code name: Resident-F3)

**Week:** 2 · **Asker:** BT · **Logger:** GU · **Consent:** yes; notes only, no recording  
**Real or practice:** real

**Context:** 4th-year CS student living in Block F, does laundry twice a week, usually late in the evening.
**Our prediction:** We are wrong if students tell us they almost always find an open machine on their first visit, or that checking machine status is not something they care about.

**Quotes** (verbatim Georgian as spoken; English beside it; ★ = surprise)
- "ორშაბათს საღამოს ჩავედი და ოთხივე მანქანა დაკავებული დამხვდა, 50 წუთი ეწერა ტაიმერზე." / "I went down Monday evening and all four machines were taken, timer showed 50 minutes."
- "ტანსაცმელი ვიღაცამ გამოიღ და მტვრიან სკამზე დაყარა, ძირსაც ეყარა რამდენიმე წინდა." / "Someone took the clothes out and dumped them on a dusty chair, even dropped a few socks on the floor." ★
- "მესინჯერის ჩატში ვწერ ხოლმე 'მესამე მანქანა ვისია', მაგრამ უმეტესად არავინ პასუხობს." / "I usually write in the Messenger chat 'whose is machine 3', but mostly nobody replies."
- "სამჯერ მომიწია კიბეებზე ასვლა-ჩამოსვლა კალათით." / "I had to go up and down the stairs three times with the basket." ★

**Workaround:** Checks manually by walking down 3 flights of stairs, or asks in the general dorm Telegram chat (which usually gets ignored).
**Cost:** 45 minutes wasted waiting/walking, dirty socks from table, high frustration.
**Frequency:** Happens almost every Sunday or Monday evening.
**Prediction check:** Confirmed. He raised the issue of empty trips and full machines immediately without us prompting it.
**Surprise ★:** The biggest anger wasn't just the waiting time—it was people touching wet laundry and dumping it onto dirty tables.
**Commitment:** Gave us the contact of his roommate on the 3rd floor; agreed we can follow up with him.
**Next:** In Interview 02, ask specifically how people feel about someone else unloading their laundry if a cycle is finished.
