Interview 03 · Resident, Block C 4th Floor · (code name: Resident-C402)

**Week:** 2 · **Asker:** GU · **Logger:** AK · **Consent:** yes; notes only, no recording

**Real or practice:** real

**Context:** 3rd-year Management student living in Block C, does laundry once a week, usually on Sunday afternoons. Our prediction: We are wrong if students think it is acceptable to leave finished laundry inside a machine for more than 15–20 minutes, or if they prefer human communication over an automated digital status tracker.

**Quotes (verbatim Georgian as spoken; English beside it; ★ = surprise)**

"15 წუთზე მეტი თუ რჩება სარეცხი გაჩერებულ მანქანაში, უკვე პრობლემაა. სხვასაც ეჩქარება." / "If clothes stay in a stopped machine for more than 15 minutes, it's already a problem. Other people are in a hurry too."

"ერთხელ 40 წუთი ველოდე პატრონს. მერე მე თვითონ გავამოღე, მაგრამ პარკში ჩავალაგე და მაგიდაზე ისე დავდე, რომ არ დასვრილიყო." / "Once I waited 40 minutes for the owner. Then I took it out myself, but put it inside a plastic bag on the table so it wouldn't get dirty." ★

"ტელეგრამში წერას აზრი არ აქვს, სულ იგნორია ან ჩატი ისპამება. აპლიკაცია ან მარტივი საიტი ჯობია, სადაც პირდაპირ გამოჩნდება ტაიმერი." / "Writing in Telegram makes no sense, it gets completely ignored or spammed. An app or simple site is better, where the timer shows directly."

"ყველაზე მეტად ის მიშლის ნერვებს, რომ არ იცი, როდის განთავისუფლდება — შეიძლება 5 წუთში ან 1 საათში." / "What annoys me most is not knowing when it will free up — it could be in 5 minutes or in 1 hour." ★

**Workaround:** Keeps a personal plastic basket specifically to move finished laundry safely if needed; sets phone alarms and checks status manually by walking down.

**Cost:** Spent over an hour waiting near the laundry room, delayed study schedule, friction with other residents.

**Frequency:** Occurs almost every weekend, particularly Sunday afternoons between 2:00 PM and 6:00 PM.

**Prediction check:** Confirmed. The resident considers leaving laundry in a completed machine for over 15 minutes unreasonable and strongly prefers digital status tracking over chat groups.

**Surprise ★:** Residents actively develop their own courtesy protocols (like using plastic bags as clean barriers) when forced to unload someone else's laundry, showing that hygiene during unloading—not the unloading itself—is the primary pain point.

**Commitment:** Agreed to test an early paper prototype/mockup of the digital status board later this week.

**Next:** In Interview 04, explore whether residents would be willing to manually update/scan a status marker at the machine when starting or ending a load.
